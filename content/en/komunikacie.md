# Communications outage, cyberattack and payments {#komunikacie}

The mobile network, the internet, payment systems and digital public services have become the foundation of everyday life. They can fail because of a power cut, a technical fault, network overload, sabotage or a cyber attack. As a result you may be unable to call your family, pay by card, withdraw money, get a prescription or find information.

## What you need to know {#komunikacie-vediet}

- **Network overload** is common right after a major event – everyone calls at once. Text messages and short messages often get through; calls do not.
- During a **power cut**, mobile masts usually last only a few hours on backup batteries.
- **A cyber attack** can hit a bank, a hospital, a public authority, an energy supplier or your own device. Common consequences are unavailable services and data leaks.
- **Payment cards and mobile payments** need both electricity and a connection. Cash is the only means of payment that always works.
- **The 112 emergency line** has priority on the network and connects even through another operator.

## Before an outage: prepare {#komunikacie-pred}

{{before:comms}}

**In addition:**

- **Contacts on paper** – family, doctor, school, employer, insurer, neighbours. In a crisis most people cannot even remember their partner's number.
- **A battery radio** and a note of the frequencies of the regional station and public broadcaster.
- **Offline maps** on your phone (downloaded areas) and a printed map.
- **Cash** in small denominations for 1–2 weeks, split between several places. Money kept in more than one bank.
- **Medicines** – do not leave prescriptions to the last minute; e-prescriptions may not be available.
- **Cyber hygiene:** updates, strong and unique passwords, a password manager, two-factor authentication, an offline data backup (external drive), caution with links and attachments.

## During the outage {#komunikacie-pocas}

- **Save battery** – lower the brightness, close unnecessary apps and mobile data, use power-saving mode.
- **Send short messages** and agree on times when you will be online.
- If nothing works: **go to the agreed meeting place** at the agreed time, or to the municipality's information point.
- **Do not respond to unverified requests** to transfer money, "verify your account" and the like – fraudsters exploit the confusion.
- **Do not withdraw large amounts of cash from banks** – mass withdrawals cause problems for everyone. Banks have outage plans.

### If your device or account has been attacked {#komunikacie-utok}

1. **Disconnect** the device from the internet (Wi-Fi, cable, mobile data).
2. **Pay nothing** to the attackers (extortion, ransomware).
3. **From another, clean device**, change the passwords for your email, bank and important accounts and turn on two-factor authentication.
4. **Block your payment cards** with your bank if they may be misused.
5. **Report** the incident – to your bank, the police and your national cyber-security centre (CSIRT).
6. Have the device **cleaned or reinstalled**, and restore your data from the backup.
