# Source library and handbook ratings {#zdroje}

This handbook is based on official guides and recommendations from national authorities, international organisations and professional bodies. We gave priority to materials that are **highly rated** for their quality – sent to millions of households, repeatedly updated, tested in practice or praised for their clarity and design.

## How we rated them {#zdroje-hodnotenie}

We rated each source on a scale of 1–5 against five criteria:

- **Completeness** – does it cover all important types of crisis and the needs of different groups?
- **Clarity** – is the language clear, the structure easy to follow, and does the design support quick orientation?
- **Practicality** – does it contain specific steps, lists, quantities?
- **Accessibility** – is it available in several languages, in easy-read, audio, sign language, and in print and digital form?
- **Currency** – does it reflect today's threats (war, cyber attacks, disinformation, climate change)?

The rating is **editorial** – it expresses how well the source served as a basis for this handbook; it is not an official assessment.

## What follows from this {#zdroje-zaver}

- **The best-rated guides** (Swedish, Japanese, German, Czech, Norwegian) share brevity, a clear "before – during – after" structure, checklists and availability in several languages and formats.
- **The recommended period of self-sufficiency** in Europe ranges from 72 hours (EU, Czechia, Finland, France, the Netherlands) to 7 days (Sweden, Norway, Switzerland, Estonia), 10 days (Germany) and 14 days (Austria). This handbook recommends **starting with 72 hours and gradually moving to 7–14 days**.
- **Water** – most sources recommend 3 litres per person per day for drinking and cooking; Germany's BBK 2 litres. The app lets you choose the standard.
- **The EU Preparedness Union Strategy** (2025) asks all member states to promote at least 72 hours of self-sufficiency; more and more countries are publishing household guides. Slovak official materials are technically sound (especially on sheltering and warning) but scattered; a single modern household guide and a public shelter map are still missing – this handbook tries to fill that gap.

## Other useful sources {#zdroje-dalsie}

- **Meteoalarm** (meteoalarm.org) – weather warnings for the whole of Europe.
- **European Flood Awareness System – EFAS** and the **Copernicus Emergency Management Service** (emergency.copernicus.eu) – maps of floods, fires and other disasters.
- **ECDC** (ecdc.europa.eu) – the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control.
- **WHO Europe** (who.int/europe) – health in emergencies, heat-health action.
- **IFRC and national Red Cross societies** – first-aid courses, preparedness, humanitarian aid.
- **ENISA** (enisa.europa.eu) and your national CSIRT – cyber security.
- **OpenStreetMap** (openstreetmap.org) – the open map data used in the app; if you know of a shelter that is missing from the map, you can add it.
