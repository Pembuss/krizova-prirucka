# Water outage and contamination {#vypadok-vody}

Tap water can stop flowing because of a burst main, a power cut (pumps), drought, frost or a flood. It can also keep flowing but be unsafe to drink – after a flood, when the source is contaminated or when treatment fails. In that case the water company and the public-health authority issue specific instructions.

{{now:water}}

## What you need to know {#vypadok-vody-vediet}

**Types of restriction:**

| Notice | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Boil water notice** | Boil water for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, washing dishes and making ice (a rolling boil for at least 1 minute). It can be used for showering and laundry. |
| **Do not drink – do not use for cooking** | Boiling is not enough (for example chemical contamination). Use bottled water or water from tankers for drinking and cooking. The tap water can be used for flushing and sometimes for washing – follow the instructions. |
| **Do not use** | Not even for washing; contact may harm your health. Rare. |

- **Boiling** reliably kills bacteria, viruses and parasites but **does not remove chemicals**, heavy metals or oil products.
- When the supply is interrupted, municipalities and water companies set up **distribution points with tankers**. Take clean containers with lids.
- **Flushing a toilet** takes about 4–6 litres of water. If the sewer is not working (flood), do not flush – use an emergency toilet.

## During the outage {#vypadok-vody-pocas}

**Use water in order of priority:**

1. **Drinking** – an adult needs 2–3 l a day, more in hot weather. Children and babies come first.
2. **Preparing food and infant formula.** Make up formula only with bottled water suitable for babies or with boiled water.
3. **Hand hygiene** – especially before eating and after using the toilet. If you do not have enough water, use hand sanitiser.
4. **Washing dishes** – use disposable dishes, or wash in one bowl and rinse in another.
5. **Flushing the toilet** – only with grey water (from the bath, rainwater), or set up an emergency toilet.

**Where to get water when your supply runs out:**

- **Water in the house:** the hot-water cylinder (close the inlet and drain it from the bottom valve), the pipes (open the highest and then the lowest tap in the house), ice cubes, tinned fruit and vegetables.
- **Tankers and municipal distribution points.**
- **Natural sources** (well, spring, stream, rainwater) – only after treatment ([Water treatment](#sebestacnost-voda)).

::: warning
**Do not drink water from radiators, the heating system, a cistern containing cleaning products, or a pool with chemicals.** Do not drink untreated water from streams and rivers – even if it looks clean, it may contain bacteria and parasites.
:::
