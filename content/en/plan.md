# Family emergency plan {#plan}

A crisis rarely finds the whole family at home. Parents are at work, children at school, grandma in another town – and the phones are down. A family plan makes sure everyone knows **what to do, where to go and how the others will learn that they are safe**, even when you cannot reach each other. A plan takes one evening and is the cheapest and most effective part of preparedness.

::: urgent
**Act now – a plan in 15 minutes**

- Agree on **three meeting places**: outside the house, in the neighbourhood, and outside the town.
- Choose a **contact person outside the area** whom everyone will report to.
- Agree on **who picks up the children** and who looks after older relatives and pets.
- Write the important numbers on paper and put a copy in everyone's wallet and school bag.
- Show all adults where the **main shut-offs** for water, gas and electricity are.
:::

## What the plan should contain {#plan-obsah}

### 1. Contacts {#plan-kontakty}

- All household members (mobile, work, school).
- **A contact person outside the area** – a relative or friend in another town or country.
- Neighbours you can turn to, and neighbours who need help.
- GP, paediatrician, pharmacy, vet.
- School, nursery, employer.
- Town hall (crisis team), fault lines of the electricity distributor, gas and water companies.
- Insurance company (policy number).
- Emergency numbers: **112** and your national numbers (in Slovakia **150, 155, 158**).

### 2. Meeting places {#plan-miesta}

| Place | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **By the house** | fire, earthquake, gas leak | the tree at the corner of the street, opposite the entrance |
| **In the neighbourhood** | you cannot return home, communication is down | school entrance, church, library |
| **Outside the town** | evacuation, large-scale crisis | relatives' house, cottage, a town in another district |

Agree on a **time** for each place as well: for example, "if we can't reach each other, every day at 10:00 and 18:00 at the neighbourhood meeting place".

### 3. Tasks {#plan-ulohy}

Divide tasks according to where each person usually is:

- who picks up the children from school or nursery (and who is the backup),
- who helps older parents or a neighbour with a disability,
- who takes the pets,
- who takes the grab bag, medicines and documents,
- who turns off the gas, water and electricity.

::: tip
Find out what **emergency plan the school and nursery have** and to whom they may release your child. If someone else might pick up the children (a grandparent, a neighbour), give the school a written authorisation in advance.
:::

### 4. Health {#plan-zdravie}

For each household member: diagnoses, regular medicines and doses, allergies, medical aids, contact details of the doctor and health insurer. This information also belongs on the **wallet card** and in your phone's emergency app or Medical ID.

### 5. Home {#plan-domov}

- Where the main **water shut-off**, **gas shut-off** and **main circuit breaker** are – and how to close them (you may need a key).
- Where the **fire extinguisher**, first-aid kit, torches, radio and supplies are.
- Which room is best for **sheltering** (cellar, an inner room without windows) – see [Sheltering](#ukrytie).
- Where the spare keys are and who has them.

### 6. Sheltering and evacuation {#plan-ukryt}

- Which **shelter the municipality has assigned** to your building and how to get there. Ask at the town hall or district office.
- Where you will go if you evacuate (relatives, cottage, accommodation) and **two or three different routes** – at least one without bridges and low-lying sections. The app will suggest them for you ([Evacuation](#evakuacia)).
- Where the municipality's **evacuation assembly point** is.

### 7. Money and documents {#plan-doklady}

Where the original documents and the copies are kept, and who has access to cash and accounts. A list of documents is in the chapter [Grab bag and documents](#batozina).

### 8. Communication {#plan-spojenie}

The frequencies of the regional radio and the public broadcaster, the PMR walkie-talkie channel, the times you will switch on your phones, and where you leave messages (door, letterbox, contact person). See [Warning and communication](#varovanie-spojenie).

## How to put the plan together {#plan-postup}

1. **Family meeting.** Involve the children too – in an age-appropriate way. Children who know what to do are less afraid.
2. **Fill in the template** in the chapter [Templates](#sablony) or in the app.
3. **Print** the plan: one copy for the fridge, one for the grab bag, wallet cards for purses and school bags.
4. **Practise** – once a year, walk the route to the meeting place and to the shelter, and test how fast you can pack the grab bag.
5. **Update** – whenever phone numbers, school, work or address change, and at the regular review twice a year.

## If you live alone {#plan-sam}

- Agree with a **neighbour or friend to check on each other** (a "buddy"): in a crisis you contact each other by an agreed time.
- Leave a **spare key** with your contact person, together with information about your medicines and health.
- If you need help to evacuate (mobility, sight, hearing), tell the **town hall** in advance – municipalities keep lists of people who need assistance.
