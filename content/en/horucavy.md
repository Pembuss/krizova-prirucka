# Heatwaves and drought {#horucavy}

Heatwaves are the deadliest natural hazard in Europe – in summer 2022 they are estimated to have caused more than 60,000 deaths. They are most dangerous for older people, small children, the chronically ill, people living alone and those who work outdoors. Prolonged drought also brings water shortages and a risk of fires.

## What you need to know {#horucavy-vediet}

- A **tropical day** has a maximum temperature of 30 °C or more; on a **tropical night** the temperature does not drop below 20 °C. Hot nights are especially dangerous because the body has no chance to recover.
- National weather services issue **high-temperature warnings** in three levels, and public-health authorities publish advice during heatwaves (thresholds differ by country, typically from about 31–33 °C).
- Cities are hotter than the countryside (the **urban heat island**) – attic flats and flats in tower blocks facing south or west overheat the most.
- Some **medicines** (for blood pressure, diuretics, psychiatric drugs, drugs for Parkinson's disease and others) reduce the body's ability to cope with heat or increase water loss. Ask your doctor.

### How to recognise overheating {#horucavy-priznaky}

| Condition | Symptoms | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Heat exhaustion** | heavy sweating, weakness, headache, dizziness, nausea, muscle cramps, rapid pulse | move to a cool place, lie down with legs raised, drink water with minerals, cool with wet compresses. If there is no improvement within an hour, call a doctor. |
| **Heatstroke** | body temperature above 40 °C, hot red skin (may be dry), confusion, slurred speech, seizures, unconsciousness | **call 112**, move the person to a cool place, undress them and **cool them aggressively** – immersion in cold water, wet sheets and a fan, ice on the neck, armpits and groin. If conscious, give sips of water. |
| **Sunstroke** | headache, nausea, red scalp, stiff neck after time in the sun | move to the shade, cool the head and neck, drink; if it gets worse, call a doctor |

## During a heatwave {#horucavy-pocas}

- **Drink** 2–3 litres a day (more during exertion), in small amounts. Older people often do not feel thirsty – they should drink according to a schedule. Urine should be pale.
- **Eat light**: vegetables, fruit, cold soups, smaller portions. Take care with storing food – it spoils quickly in the heat.
- **Your home:** keep windows closed during the day and shade them from outside (external blinds are up to 5 times more effective than inside curtains). Ventilate at night and early in the morning, use cross-draughts. Switch off appliances you do not need.
- **Cooling your body:** a lukewarm shower, a wet towel on your neck, feet in water, a fan at temperatures up to 35 °C. If it is above 32 °C indoors, spend at least a few hours in an air-conditioned place.
- **Clothing:** loose, light-coloured, natural fabrics, a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen.
- **Physical exertion** only in the morning and evening. When working outdoors, take a break in the shade every hour.
- **Never leave** a child, an older person or an animal in a car – the temperature inside rises by more than 10 °C in 10 minutes.
- **Check** on older and isolated neighbours twice a day.
- **Pets:** water and shade, walks early in the morning and late in the evening; hot asphalt burns paws.

## Drought and water shortage {#horucavy-sucho}

- Respect the **restrictions** of the municipality and water company (bans on watering gardens, filling pools, washing cars).
- Save water: shorter showers, full loads in the washing machine and dishwasher, collecting rainwater for the garden.
- Observe **bans on lighting fires** outdoors and do not throw away cigarette ends. Dry grass and forests ignite very easily ([Fires](#poziare)).
