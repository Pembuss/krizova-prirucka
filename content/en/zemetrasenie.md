# Earthquakes and landslides {#zemetrasenie}

Central Europe is not a region of strong earthquakes, but it is not risk-free either. Historically destructive shocks hit Komárno in Slovakia (1763), Žilina (1858) and the Dobrá Voda area in the Little Carpathians (1906), and weaker tremors occur every year. Landslides are much more common – after long periods of rain they threaten houses and roads in hilly areas. If you travel to Italy, Greece, Turkey, Croatia, Slovenia, Albania or Romania, the earthquake risk is considerably higher.

## What you need to know {#zemetrasenie-vediet}

- **Earthquakes cannot be predicted.** Shaking usually lasts from a few seconds to a few tens of seconds. The main shock is followed by **aftershocks**, which can bring down damaged buildings.
- **Most injuries** are caused by falling objects, broken glass and trying to run during the shaking – not by entire buildings collapsing.
- **Doorways** are not safer (in modern buildings they are no stronger than the rest). It is better to get under a sturdy table.
- **Landslides** are heralded by cracks in the ground, walls and asphalt, leaning trees and poles, water seeping out of the ground, cracking sounds, and doors and windows that jam.

## Before an earthquake: prepare {#zemetrasenie-pred}

{{before:quake}}

**In addition:**

- Do not hang heavy pictures or shelves above the bed.
- Store glass and heavy items on lower shelves, and fit latches to cupboards.
- Practise **"Drop – Cover – Hold on"** with the children.
- In landslide areas, watch the condition of the slope and the drainage of your land; do not pile soil or let water collect on the slope.

## During the shaking {#zemetrasenie-pocas}

- **At home:** drop to the floor, get under a sturdy table, hold on to its leg and protect your head and neck. If there is no table, crouch by an inner wall and protect your head with your arms. Keep away from windows, wardrobes and light fittings. **Stay inside** until the shaking stops.
- **In bed:** stay lying down and protect your head with a pillow.
- **Outdoors:** move to an open space away from buildings, poles, trees and power lines.
- **In a car:** stop away from bridges, underpasses and buildings, and stay in the car with your seatbelt on.
- **In a shop, cinema or stadium:** do not rush to the exits; take cover and protect your head, then leave calmly.

## After the shaking {#zemetrasenie-po}

{{after:quake}}

**In addition:**

- Put on **sturdy shoes** – there may be broken glass on the floor.
- Check the **gas, water and electricity**; if you smell gas, open the windows, leave and call the emergency line. Do not light a flame.
- If there are large cracks in the building, **leave by the stairs** and wait for a structural engineer to assess it.
- On the coast (Mediterranean), after strong shaking **move away from the sea to higher ground** – there is a risk of a tsunami.
- Listen to the radio and follow instructions. Make phone calls only in emergencies – the network will be overloaded.

## Landslides {#zemetrasenie-zosuvy}

- If you notice signs of a landslide, **report it to the municipality** and the national geological survey (it keeps a register of landslides).
- During an active landslide, **leave the building** and move away **at right angles to the direction of movement**. Warn your neighbours.
- After a landslide, do not return to the area until experts have assessed it – further movement and damaged utility lines are a risk.
