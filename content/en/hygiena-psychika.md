# Hygiene, sanitation and mental health {#hygiena-psychika}

In a prolonged crisis, **diseases spread by dirty hands, water and waste** and **exhaustion from stress** threaten health more than the event itself. This chapter shows how to stay clean without running water, how to set up an emergency toilet, how to handle waste – and how to look after your own mental health and that of those close to you.

::: urgent
**Act now – hygiene without water**

- **Wash your hands** before eating, before preparing food, after using the toilet and after caring for a sick person – with soap and a little water, or with sanitiser.
- If flushing or the sewer is not working, **set up an emergency toilet** (bucket, bags, absorbent material).
- Put **rubbish** in strong closed bags outside living areas.
- Drink only **bottled, boiled or treated water**.
- Without a fridge, eat opened **food** as soon as possible.
:::

## Personal hygiene with minimal water {#hygiena-osobna}

- **Hand washing:** fix soap on a string to a canister or bottle with a tap and put a basin underneath. About 0.5 litres of water is enough to wash your hands. Between washes use sanitiser with at least 60 % alcohol.
- **Body:** wash in sections with a flannel or wet wipes – face first, then the body, and finally intimate areas and feet. 2–3 litres of warm water are enough for a full wash.
- **Teeth:** with drinking or boiled water – a glass is enough.
- **Hair:** dry shampoo, or wash once every few days.
- **Menstruation:** enough pads and tampons, or a menstrual cup (needs clean water for sterilising).
- **Laundry:** keep plenty of underwear and socks; wash in a little water with soap and rinse several times in small amounts.
- **Dishes:** disposable dishes save water; otherwise wash in one bowl and rinse in a second with a few drops of chlorine product.

## Emergency toilet {#hygiena-zachod}

If flushing does not work but the sewer is fine, you can flush the toilet with **a bucket of grey water** (4–6 litres) – pour it into the bowl all at once. If **the sewer is not working** (flood, fault), do not flush at all – the waste could back up into flats on lower floors.

**How to set up an emergency toilet:**

1. Put **two strong rubbish bags** into the toilet bowl (emptied and with the water supply turned off) or into a bucket with a lid.
2. Have **absorbent material** ready – sawdust, cat litter, peat, dry soil, paper – and a **scoop**.
3. After each use, cover the faeces with a layer of absorbent (about a cupful). You can collect urine separately in a bottle or container – it smells less and the bag lasts longer.
4. When the bag is about half full, **tie it**, put it in another bag and store it in a closed container outside the flat (balcony, cellar, yard) until it is collected.
5. **Wash your hands** after each use.

::: tip
A family of four needs roughly 10–15 litres of absorbent material and 10–20 bags per week. The app calculates the amount for your household.
:::

## Waste and cleanliness {#hygiena-odpad}

- **Sorting**: keep food waste separate in a closed container (it smells quickly and attracts insects and rodents); keep the rest dry.
- Store rubbish **outside living areas**, protected from animals and rain.
- **Disinfection:** clean door handles, switches, the toilet and kitchen surfaces often. Dilute chlorine products according to the instructions and never mix them with other cleaning products (a toxic gas is produced).
- **Food:** transfer opened tins into a clean container; without refrigeration do not keep leftovers until the next day (especially meat, rice and dairy). Keep flies and rodents away with closed containers.

## Mental health in a crisis {#hygiena-psychika-zdravie}

A crisis is a burden on the mind. Fear, restlessness, insomnia, irritability, sadness, headaches or stomach aches, difficulty concentrating and a sense of helplessness are **normal reactions to an abnormal situation**. For most people they gradually fade. What helps is anything that gives a sense of control and belonging.

### What helps {#hygiena-psychika-pomaha}

- **A plan and activity** – do at least one useful thing a day (check supplies, help a neighbour). Acting reduces anxiety.
- **A daily routine** – getting up, meals and sleep at the same times, even in a shelter.
- **Limit the news** – follow verified information two or three times a day, not continuously.
- **Contact with loved ones** – talking about feelings, eating together, humour.
- **Exercise** – a walk, exercises even in the flat. Breathing: breathe in slowly for 4 counts and out for 6 counts for a few minutes.
- **Help others** – helping others strengthens a sense of purpose.
- **Avoid alcohol and sedatives** without consulting a doctor – they worsen sleep and judgement.

### Psychological first aid for others {#hygiena-psychika-ppp}

According to WHO guidance, three steps are enough: **Look – Listen – Link.**

1. **Look:** is the person safe? Do they have their basic needs (water, warmth, medicines)? Are they in acute danger (unconscious, suicidal thoughts)?
2. **Listen:** approach calmly, introduce yourself, ask what they need. Do not force them to talk, do not judge, do not comfort with clichés ("it'll be fine").
3. **Link:** help them get practical help, information and contact with loved ones or professionals.

### Children in a crisis {#hygiena-psychika-deti}

- Children take their cue from **adults' reactions** – try to stay calm, even if you are afraid.
- **Explain truthfully and in an age-appropriate way**, briefly and clearly. Tell them what you are doing to keep them safe.
- Expect **regression** (thumb-sucking, bedwetting, clinging to a parent), nightmares, anger. These are common reactions.
- Keep up **rituals** (bedtime story, family meals), give them **tasks** (their own bag, caring for a pet) and room for play and drawing.
- Limit their exposure to **dramatic news and videos**.

### When to seek professional help {#hygiena-psychika-pomoc}

If difficulties last longer than **4 weeks**, get worse or stop you functioning, or if you or someone close to you has **thoughts of suicide**, seek help:

- **116 123** – the harmonised European number for emotional support helplines (available in many EU countries, free of charge).
- **116 111** – the European helpline for children and young people.
- In Slovakia: **Linka dôvery Nezábudka** 0800 800 566 (24/7, free); in Czechia: **Linka první psychické pomoci** 116 123.
- Your GP, a psychologist or a mental-health crisis centre.
- If a life is in immediate danger: **112**.
