# Shelters and refuges in Europe {#kryty-europa}

European countries protect their populations in very different ways. **Switzerland and Finland** have built shelters for decades and have room for most of their inhabitants. **Sweden, Norway and Denmark** have extensive shelter networks and are renewing them. **Central and Western Europe** mostly retain Cold War shelters in varying condition, and many countries rely on sheltering in buildings, cellars, underground car parks and metro stations. Since 2022 most countries have been reviewing their systems: Poland has introduced compulsory "temporary shelter places" in new buildings, Germany is mapping "public refuges" and Norway has reinstated the obligation to build shelters.

**There is no single European shelter database.** This chapter therefore gives an overview of the system in each country with links to official maps and information. The app and the website also show shelters, bunkers, assembly points and other important places from OpenStreetMap open data anywhere in Europe.

::: warning
The information is current as of October 2026 and changes. **Before you travel, or in a crisis, check it on the official websites** listed for each country. Shelters from open data (OpenStreetMap) are incomplete, and their condition and accessibility have not been verified.
:::

## Types of refuge {#kryty-europa-druhy}

| Type | Description | Where it is found |
|---|---|---|
| **Blast-resistant civil-defence shelter** | protects against blast, radiation and chemical agents, filtered ventilation | CH, FI, SE, NO, DK, SK, CZ, PL, UA, IL |
| **Gas-tight shelter** | sealed, protects against radiation and chemicals | SK, CZ, PL |
| **Simple or improvised shelter** | an adapted cellar, basement or hallway | all of Europe; SK (JÚBS), CZ (improvised shelters), PL (temporary shelter places) |
| **Public refuge** | underground stations, car parks, underpasses, basements of public buildings | DE (planned register), PL, CZ (Prague metro), HU (Budapest metro), UA |
| **Safe room in the home** | a reinforced room in every flat | IL (mamad), partly FI |
| **Evacuation centre and emergency accommodation** | schools, gyms, hostels for evacuees | all countries |
| **Warming, cooling and help points** | heat, electricity, water, internet, information during outages | UA (invincibility points), SE (trygghetspunkter), DE (Notfalltreffpunkte), many cities |
| **Assembly area** | open space after an earthquake or during evacuation | IT (aree di attesa), GR, TR, cities across Europe |

## How to find a refuge in another country {#kryty-europa-hladanie}

1. **Listen to local instructions** – radio, mobile alerts (EU-Alert), announcements, hotel staff.
2. **Look at the country's official map** (links below) or the local civil-protection app.
3. **Look for signs** – the international civil-defence emblem (a blue equilateral triangle on an orange background) and words such as *Schutzraum*, *Skyddsrum*, *Väestönsuoja*, *Tilfluktsrom*, *Schron*, *Úkryt*, *Óvóhely*, *Sklonište*, *Adăpost*, *Укриття*.
4. **If you find nothing** – the nearest solid building, cellar, underground car park or metro station; otherwise the two-wall rule ([Sheltering](#ukrytie-dve-steny)).

## The map in the app {#kryty-europa-mapa}

For any chosen location in Europe, the map in the app and on the website shows:

- **shelters and bunkers** tagged in OpenStreetMap as air-raid or civil-defence shelters,
- **assembly points** for emergencies,
- **underground car parks and metro stations** as possible improvised refuges,
- **hospitals, pharmacies, fire stations, police, town halls**,
- **drinking-water sources** (fountains, wells) and **petrol stations**,
- **your own places** – the shelter assigned by your municipality, a meeting place, relatives.

For Slovakia the map, these places and the **civil-defence shelters from published municipal lists** are built into the app and work **without internet**. For other countries nearby places are loaded when you are online and saved on your device. You can also print a map of your surroundings.
