# Home supplies and rotation {#zasoby}

Home supplies allow you to stay at home when shops, water, electricity or transport are not working, or when it is dangerous to go outside. This is not "hoarding" but a sensible reserve that you use every day and replenish regularly. Specific shopping lists for different households are in the chapter [Shopping lists](#nakupne-zoznamy). The app calculates them exactly for your family.

::: urgent
**Act now – when a crisis is coming and you have no supplies**

- Fill clean bottles, pots and canisters with tap water (allow 3 l per person per day) and fill the bath with water for washing and flushing.
- Buy **water, no-cook food, medicines, batteries and cash** first – in that order.
- Collect your regular prescription medicines.
- Charge phones, power banks, laptops and cordless tools.
- Fill up the car.
- Do not buy more than you need – others need supplies too.
:::

## Water – the most important supply {#zasoby-voda}

People can survive weeks without food but only a few days without water. During a power cut the pumps in apartment buildings often stop; during a flood tap water may become unsafe to drink.

**How much water:**

- **Drinking and cooking: 3 litres per person per day.** This is recommended by Denmark, Sweden and most other countries. Germany's BBK works with a minimum of 2 litres (1.5 l for drinking, 0.5 l for cooking). More is needed in hot weather, during physical exertion, breastfeeding and illness.
- **Hygiene: at least 1–2 litres per person per day** extra (washing hands, teeth, dishes). For flushing the toilet use bath water or rainwater, or an emergency toilet ([Hygiene](#hygiena-psychika)).
- **Children** need less (about half); **formula-fed babies** need water to prepare the formula. **Pets:** a dog needs about 50 ml per kilogram of body weight per day, a cat about 0.25 l.

**How to store water:**

- **Bottled water** is the easiest – it usually keeps for a year or more. Store it somewhere cool and dark, away from chemicals.
- **Tap water** in clean food-grade containers (PET bottles, canisters) keeps for about **6 months**. Fill the containers to the brim, close them and write the date on them.
- Also keep **empty collapsible canisters** for water from a tanker.
- How to treat water when your supply runs out is explained in the chapter [Self-reliance](#sebestacnost-voda).

## Food {#zasoby-jedlo}

### Principles {#zasoby-zasady}

1. **Long-life and no refrigeration.** Tins, dried and packaged food that does not need a fridge.
2. **At least half no-cook.** During a power and gas outage you must be able to eat without cooking: crispbread, tinned fish and meat, beans, sweetcorn, ready meals in tins, peanut butter, nuts, dried fruit, UHT milk, muesli, bars, chocolate. If you cook with electricity, the share of no-cook food should be even higher.
3. **What you normally eat.** A crisis is no time to experiment with your stomach. Think of diets, allergies, children and pets too.
4. **Energy: about 2,000–2,200 kcal per adult per day.** Children and older people need less; teenagers, pregnant and breastfeeding women need more.
5. **Low salt and low water needs.** Very salty food makes you thirsty.
6. **Small packs.** Once opened without a fridge, you can finish the whole pack quickly.
7. **Do not forget a tin opener** (a manual one!), matches and a stove.

### Good things to store {#zasoby-vhodne}

- **Cereals and sides:** rice, pasta, couscous, oats, muesli, crispbread, rice cakes, instant mashed potato, flour.
- **Pulses and vegetables:** beans, chickpeas, lentils, peas, tinned sweetcorn and tomatoes, jarred vegetables, tinned ready meals.
- **Fruit:** tinned fruit, dried fruit, fruit juices, pouches for children.
- **Dairy:** UHT milk, powdered milk, hard cheese, plant-based drinks.
- **Protein:** tuna, sardines, tinned meat, pâté, soy chunks.
- **Fats:** oil, olives.
- **Nuts and spreads:** peanut butter, nuts, seeds.
- **Sweet and other:** honey, sugar, chocolate, cereal bars, tea, coffee, salt, spices, instant soups, multivitamins.

## Medicines and medical supplies {#zasoby-lieky}

- Keep a supply of your regular medicines for at least **30 days** (Swedish recommendation). Arrange prescriptions with your doctor in good time and do not wait until the last tablet.
- Check how your medicines should be stored if refrigeration fails. Many types of insulin keep at room temperature for a limited time (usually several weeks) – the exact information is in the package leaflet; ask your doctor or pharmacist. Keep a cool bag and ice packs.
- Complete your home first-aid kit ([First aid](#prva-pomoc)) with medicines for pain, fever, diarrhoea and allergy, oral rehydration salts, a thermometer and FFP2 respirators.

## Hygiene, light, heat and other items {#zasoby-ostatne}

- **Hygiene:** toilet paper, wet wipes, hand sanitiser, soap, menstrual products, nappies, rubbish bags, a bucket with a lid and absorbent material for an emergency toilet.
- **Light and power:** a head torch or torch for everyone, spare batteries, power banks, a battery or wind-up radio, candles and matches (with care).
- **Cooking and heat:** a camping gas stove with fuel (only outdoors or with good ventilation), blankets, sleeping bags, warm clothing, vacuum flasks – details in the chapter [Power and essentials by scenario](#energie-scenare).
- **Safety:** smoke alarm, carbon monoxide (CO) alarm, fire extinguisher and fire blanket.
- **Tools:** duct tape, heavy plastic sheeting (for sealing windows), a multi-tool, work gloves.
- **Cash:** in small notes and coins – enough for about 1–2 weeks of essential spending.

## Where to store supplies {#zasoby-sklad}

- **Cool, dry and dark** – ideally 10–20 °C. Heat shortens shelf life.
- **Not everything in the cellar** if there is a flood risk. Keep part of it higher up.
- **Spread your supplies** – some at home, some in the car, at a cottage or with relatives.
- Protect food from pests (sturdy containers) and damp.
- Supplies for sheltering (water, food, light, blankets) can be kept directly in the cellar or the room you will use for sheltering.

## Rotation: so nothing expires {#zasoby-obmena}

**The FIFO rule – "first in, first out":** put new food at the back, older food at the front, and use the older food first. That way your supply stays fresh and you throw nothing away.

- Write the **purchase date** on packs and make the **expiry date** clearly visible.
- A **"best before" date** means the food is often still safe after it if the packaging is intact. A **"use by" date** must be observed strictly.
- **Throw away** tins with bulging, rusty or damaged lids.
- Twice a year (for example when the clocks change), check your supplies using the [regular review](#kontrolne-zoznamy). The app keeps a rotation table and reminds you what is nearing its expiry date.

## How to build up supplies gradually {#zasoby-postupne}

You do not have to buy a two-week supply at once. Add a few extra items every time you shop. With a budget of €20–30 a week you will have a solid supply in two to three months. The app turns your budget into a week-by-week purchase plan, starting with the most important items (water, no-cook food, light, medicines) and moving to less urgent ones.
