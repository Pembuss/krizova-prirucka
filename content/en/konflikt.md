# Armed conflict and air attacks {#konflikt}

The war in Ukraine has shown that armed conflict in Europe is not a thing of the past. It is not only about fighting at the front: civilians are affected by air and missile attacks, strikes on energy and water facilities, sabotage, cyber attacks, disinformation campaigns, shortages of goods and evacuations. Membership of NATO and the EU significantly reduces the risk of a direct attack on most European countries. Yet countries such as Sweden, Finland, Poland and the Baltic states tell their citizens clearly: preparedness for war is part of defending the country.

## What you need to know {#konflikt-vediet}

- **Hybrid threats** precede or accompany conflict: cyber attacks, sabotage of infrastructure, disinformation, intimidation. Their aim is to undermine trust and create chaos ([Communications](#komunikacie), [Warning – disinformation](#varovanie-dezinformacie)).
- **A state of war or emergency** allows the state to restrict some rights (movement, assembly) and to order labour duties, evacuation, the housing of evacuees and requisitioning.
- **Air-raid warnings** use sirens followed by a spoken message – in Slovakia the 2-minute wailing **"General threat"** signal with the announcement **"Air raid alert"**, in Czechia the **"General warning"** signal. Signals differ in other countries ([Shelters in Europe](#kryty-europa)).
- **The greatest danger** in attacks comes from **glass splinters and debris**, the blast wave and fires – not a direct hit. That is why simply being away from windows, with at least two walls between you and the outside, already protects you.
- **Municipalities** are responsible for sheltering, evacuating and providing emergency supplies to the population under civil-protection plans.

## Before a conflict: prepare {#konflikt-pred}

{{before:war}}

**In addition:**

- **Supplies for 7–14 days** or more, including a month's medicines, cash and fuel.
- **Shelter** – find out where it is, and prepare your own cellar following the chapter [Sheltering](#ukrytie-domov).
- **Documents** – valid passports for the whole family (in case you need to leave the country), copies, scans.
- **A family plan**: where you will meet, who will look after the children if one parent is called up; a contact abroad.
- **Skills**: first aid including stopping massive bleeding, the basics of sheltering, cooking without electricity.
- **Get involved**: volunteer civil-protection units, the Red Cross, volunteer fire brigades, military reserves. A society that works together is more resilient.

## During an air attack {#konflikt-utok}

1. **Do not wait when the alarm sounds** – go immediately to a shelter, cellar, underground car park or metro station.
2. **If no shelter can be reached within a few minutes**, apply the **two-wall rule** – an inner hallway, bathroom, stairwell, away from windows ([Sheltering](#ukrytie-dve-steny)).
3. **Outdoors:** lie down in a ditch, behind a wall or kerb, protect your head with your arms and keep your mouth slightly open (blast wave).
4. **In a car:** stop, get out and look for shelter; a car offers no protection.
5. **Stay in the shelter** until the all-clear – attacks can come in waves.
6. **After the attack:** watch out for unexploded ordnance, damaged buildings, power lines and fires. Give first aid if it is safe, and call 112.

::: warning
**Do not photograph or post** impact sites, troop movements, air defences or the locations of shelters. Attackers use such footage to guide further strikes. Do not spread unverified reports of casualties and damage either.
:::

## Unexploded ordnance {#konflikt-municia}

- **Do not touch** any suspicious object – a mine, grenade, part of a missile, drone or unknown package.
- **Move away** the same way you came and **mark the spot** (for example with tape or stones, from a safe distance).
- **Report** the find to 112 or the police.
- Teach children not to pick anything up.

## Life in a prolonged conflict {#konflikt-zivot}

- **Power and water outages** will become more frequent – follow the chapters [Power outage](#vypadok-elektriny) and [Water outage](#vypadok-vody).
- **Supplies** may be limited or regulated (rationing). Follow the authorities' instructions ([Economic crisis](#ekonomicka)).
- **Information** – trust official sources and public-service media; be critical of reports on social media.
- **Psychological resilience** – a daily routine, cooperation with neighbours, care for children and older people ([Mental health](#hygiena-psychika)).
- **Evacuation** – follow instructions, take your grab bag and register ([Evacuation](#evakuacia)).
