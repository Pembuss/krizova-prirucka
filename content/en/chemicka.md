# Chemical and industrial accidents {#chemicka}

Dangerous substances are produced, stored and transported everywhere – in chemical plants, ice rinks (ammonia), water-treatment plants (chlorine), fertiliser stores, by rail and by road. An accident or fire can release a **cloud of toxic gas** that drifts with the wind. Taking shelter quickly in a building is usually the best protection.

## What you need to know {#chemicka-vediet}

- **Warning:** a siren (for example the 2-minute wailing "General threat" signal in Slovakia or "General warning" in Czechia) followed by a spoken message saying which substance is involved and what to do. Around industrial plants there are also local sirens and announcements; in many EU countries you will also get a mobile alert.
- **Most industrial gases are heavier than air** (chlorine, hydrogen sulphide, phosgene) – they sink to the ground, into cellars, underpasses and valleys. **Ammonia** is lighter than air and rises.
- **Warning signs:** an unusual smell (chlorine – pungent, ammonia – suffocating, hydrogen sulphide – rotten eggs), burning eyes and throat, coughing, shortness of breath, nausea. Some substances have no smell.
- **Plants handling dangerous substances** (so-called Seveso establishments) must have emergency plans and inform the public nearby. Ask your municipality or regional authority.

## Before an accident: prepare {#chemicka-pred}

{{before:chemical}}

**In addition:**

- Find out whether you live or work, or your children go to school, in a plant's **emergency planning zone**, and what warning signals the plant uses.
- Keep **materials for sealing** one room ready: plastic sheeting, duct tape, towels, scissors.
- Note the number of your **national poison information centre** (for example Slovakia **+421 2 5477 4166**, Czechia **+420 224 919 293**).

## During the accident {#chemicka-pocas}

### If you are outdoors {#chemicka-vonku}

- **Go into the nearest building.** It does not have to be yours – a shop, an office, a school.
- Protect your **airways** with a damp cloth (handkerchief, scarf, towel) and cover as much skin as possible.
- If there is no building nearby, move away **at right angles to the wind** (the wind should blow from the side, not on your back or in your face), towards higher ground. Do not pass through the cloud and do not stay in hollows or underpasses.
- In a car: close the windows, switch the ventilation to recirculation or off completely, and drive away at right angles to the wind or into a building.

### In a building {#chemicka-budova}

{{actionsteps:shelterInPlace}}

- With **chlorine and other heavy gases**, move to an **upper floor**, to a room on the side facing away from the source; with **ammonia**, to a lower floor.
- Put a damp cloth over your face if you can smell the gas indoors too.
- **Do not smoke or use open flames** – some gases are flammable.
- **Do not make unnecessary phone calls**; listen to the radio.
- **Children** stay at school, which follows its own plan.

### If you have been exposed {#chemicka-zasiahnuty}

1. **Leave** the contaminated area if it is safe to do so.
2. **Remove** contaminated clothing (cut it off so you do not pull it over your head) and put it in a bag.
3. **Rinse your skin** under running lukewarm water for at least 15 minutes, and **your eyes** for at least 15 minutes (remove contact lenses).
4. If there is coughing, shortness of breath, burning eyes or unconsciousness, **call 112** and say which substance was involved.
5. **Do not induce vomiting** if the substance was swallowed; call the poison centre.

## After the accident {#chemicka-po}

{{after:chemical}}

**In addition:** do not use food and water that may have been exposed until the authorities approve it. Eat vegetables from your garden only after being told it is safe. Wash surfaces that may have come into contact with the substance with water and detergent, wearing gloves.
