# How to use this handbook – signpost {#rozcestnik}

This handbook is not a novel to read from cover to cover. It is a **tool**: in calm times it helps you prepare, and in a crisis it lets you find quickly what to do. It summarises the best-rated European and international guides (Swedish, German, Finnish, Norwegian, Danish, Swiss, Czech, American, Japanese and others) together with the instructions of Slovak civil protection. An overview and rating of the sources is in the chapter [Source library](#zdroje).

::: note
**If a crisis is happening right now,** stop reading here. Find your situation in the signpost below and go straight to the **Act now** box in the relevant chapter. If a life is at risk, call **112**.
:::

::: note
**About national details.** The handbook was written in Slovakia, so where a chapter needs a concrete example – siren signals, phone numbers, authorities, laws – it usually gives the Slovak one, often alongside Czech and other European examples. The general advice applies anywhere in Europe. For your own country, check the [country overview](#kryty-europa-krajiny) and the official websites listed there.
:::

## How the book is organised {#rozcestnik-struktura}

- **Part I – Basics of preparedness** explains what applies to every crisis: how to assess your risks, how you will learn about danger, how to put together a family plan, supplies and a grab bag, how to shelter and how to evacuate.
- **Part II – Crises step by step** has a separate chapter for each type of crisis. Each starts with an **Act now** box, followed by *what you need to know*, *how to prepare*, *what to do during the crisis* and *what to do afterwards*.
- **Part III – Health, skills and community** covers first aid, hygiene without running water, mental health, care for children, older people, people with disabilities and animals, cooking, heating and lighting without electricity, and working together with neighbours.
- **Part IV – Lists, plans and preparation** contains food shopping lists for 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, energy supply for different scenarios, printable checklists and family-plan templates.
- **Appendices** contain an overview of shelters and refuges in Europe, the library of sources, and a glossary with important numbers.

## How to start if you are not yet prepared {#rozcestnik-zacat}

Preparedness is not a one-off purchase but a few simple steps. Recommended order:

1. **Today (15 minutes):** save the emergency numbers, find out what the sirens sound like ([Warning](#varovanie)) and agree a meeting place with your family ([Family plan](#plan)).
2. **This week:** set aside water and food for 3 days ([Home supplies](#zasoby)) and print the [checklists](#kontrolne-zoznamy).
3. **This month:** pack grab bags ([Grab bag](#batozina)), find out where your shelter is ([Sheltering](#ukrytie)) and plan escape routes ([Evacuation](#evakuacia)).
4. **Within three months:** extend supplies to 7–14 days ([Shopping lists](#nakupne-zoznamy)), arrange light, heat and cooking without electricity ([Power and essentials](#energie-scenare)) and take a first-aid course.
5. **Twice a year:** check your supplies and plan using the [Regular review](#kontrolne-zoznamy) list.

## Digital tools {#rozcestnik-nastroje}

This book comes with an **app** and a **website**. In the app you describe your household (number and ages of members, housing, heating, shelter options, transport, health needs, pets, budget) and you get:

- a personal step-by-step plan and a risk profile for where you live,
- exact quantities of water, food and other supplies with indicative prices and a gradual purchase plan within your budget,
- a map of shelters, hospitals, water sources and other important places nearby,
- several suggested escape routes by car, on foot and by bike, with warnings about risky spots,
- all lists, cards and plans ready to print.

The app works offline and your data stays only on your device.

::: warning
**This handbook does not replace official instructions.** In a real crisis, the instructions of your municipality, regional authorities, the emergency services and other crisis-management bodies always take precedence. Information on shelters, signals and numbers can change – check it on official websites.
:::
