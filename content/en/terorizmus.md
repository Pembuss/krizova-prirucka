# Terrorism, violence and unrest {#terorizmus}

A terrorist attack or an active-shooter attack is rare but unfolds very quickly – seconds and minutes count before the police arrive. The proven approach from the United Kingdom and other countries can be summed up in three words: **RUN – HIDE – TELL**. It is just as important to know how to stop massive bleeding, the most common cause of death in attacks.

{{now:terror}}

## What you need to know {#terorizmus-vediet}

- **Attacks target** places with many people: squares, concerts, shopping centres, schools, transport hubs, places of worship.
- **The attacker** may use a firearm, a knife, a vehicle or explosives. An attack may have several phases or several attackers.
- **An explosion** may be followed by another one at the place where people and rescuers gather.
- **The police** first neutralise the attacker and only then treat the injured. In the first minutes, first aid is therefore often given by bystanders.

## RUN – HIDE – TELL {#terorizmus-postup}

### RUN {#terorizmus-utec}

- If you know where the threat is coming from and there is a **safe route**, run **away from the attacker**.
- **Leave your belongings.** Help others to escape, but do not let them slow you down.
- Warn others not to enter the area.

### HIDE {#terorizmus-skry}

- If you cannot run, **hide** in a room that can be locked.
- **Barricade the door** with furniture, turn off the lights, keep quiet, **switch off your phone's sound and vibration**.
- Take cover behind solid objects (walls, concrete) – plasterboard, wood or a car will not stop a bullet.
- Stay **away from doors and windows**.
- If the attacker gets in and there is no way out, as a last resort you can fight back together with others.

### TELL {#terorizmus-oznam}

- When it is safe, call **112**. If you cannot speak, leave the line open so the operator can listen.
- Say **where** you are, **how many** attackers there are, **what they look like**, what weapon they have, **where** they are moving, and how many people are injured.
- **When the police arrive:** make no sudden movements, keep your **empty hands visible** (palms open), follow instructions, do not point towards the officers and do not run towards them. The police do not know who the attacker is.

## Stop massive bleeding {#terorizmus-krvacanie}

1. **Press directly on the wound** with the palm of your hand over a dressing or cloth, using your whole body weight.
2. **Pressure bandage:** put a folded dressing on the wound, a pressure pad (rolled bandage) on top, and bandage firmly.
3. **Tourniquet** for bleeding from a limb that cannot be stopped by pressure: apply 5–8 cm above the wound (not over a joint), tighten until the bleeding stops, and note the time. Do not loosen the tourniquet.
4. **Wound on the torso or neck:** pack a dressing or cloth into the wound (wound packing) and press.
5. Keep the injured person warm and monitor their breathing.

## Suspicious object and explosion {#terorizmus-predmet}

- **Suspicious object** (an abandoned bag with wires, an unusual package): do not touch it, move at least 100 m away (behind a solid barrier), warn others and call 112. **Do not use a phone or radio nearby.**
- **After an explosion:** leave the area if it is safe, protect yourself from a further blast (keep away from cars, bins and glass), help the injured out of the danger zone, follow the rescuers' instructions.

## Violent unrest and dangerous crowds {#terorizmus-nepokoje}

- **Avoid** demonstrations that may turn violent. Follow the news and police instructions.
- If you find yourself in a crowd, **move with it diagonally** towards the edge, not against it. Hold your arms in front of your chest to keep room to breathe.
- If you fall, curl up on your side, protect your head and get up as soon as possible.
- **Do not stop** to film or take photos.
- At home: close windows and doors, put the car in the garage, keep the children at home.
