# Economic and supply crises {#ekonomicka}

An economic crisis may arrive slowly (high inflation, rising energy prices, job loss) or suddenly (payment-system outage, broken supply chains, panic buying, sanctions, war). Unlike natural disasters, it lasts not days but months or years. The best protection is a financial reserve, sensible supplies, skills and good relations with your neighbours.

{{now:economic}}

## What you need to know {#ekonomicka-vediet}

- **Panic buying** empties shelves even when there are enough goods – it only takes everyone buying three times as much. If you have supplies in advance, you do not need to join in.
- **Inflation** erodes savings held in cash and current accounts. When inflation is high, it pays to buy long-life items that you will use anyway earlier.
- **Supply disruptions** (medicines, fuel, electronics, imported food) can last weeks. States hold **strategic reserves** and in extreme cases may introduce **regulated sales or rationing**.
- **Scams** and "guaranteed" investments (cryptocurrencies, gold sold by phone, pyramid schemes) multiply in a crisis.

## Before a crisis: prepare {#ekonomicka-pred}

{{before:economic}}

**In addition:**

- **A financial reserve** covering 3–6 months of essential expenses – part in an account, part in cash at home, possibly in an account at another bank.
- **Reduce debts** with variable interest rates and unnecessary regular payments.
- **A rotating supply** of long-life food and hygiene products for a month or more ([Shopping lists](#nakupne-zoznamy)).
- **Energy efficiency** – insulation, efficient appliances, heating controls – reduces your dependence on prices.
- **Skills**: growing vegetables, preserving, cooking from basic ingredients, small repairs, sewing.
- **Community**: neighbourhood networks, swapping goods and services, buying together.

## During the crisis {#ekonomicka-pocas}

- **Budget**: list income and expenses and cut what is unnecessary. Priorities: food, housing, energy, medicines, transport to work, loan repayments.
- **Talk early** to your bank and service providers if you may not be able to pay – a repayment schedule can usually be arranged. Claim the **social support** and benefits you are entitled to.
- **Shop sensibly**: replenish supplies continuously, watch for offers, buy seasonal and local food.
- **Share and swap**: car, tools, surplus produce from the garden, childcare.
- **Beware of scams**: never give anyone access to your account, do not invest under pressure, check offers.
- If **rationing** is introduced, follow the authorities' instructions and do not fall for alarmist rumours.
