# Epidemics and pandemics {#pandemia}

An epidemic is an unusually rapid spread of an infectious disease in a particular area; a pandemic is its spread worldwide. COVID-19 showed that a pandemic is not only a health crisis: it also brings closed schools and borders, supply shortages, overloaded hospitals, isolation and economic problems. Experts say further pandemics (influenza, new viruses) are only a matter of time.

{{now:pandemic}}

## What you need to know {#pandemia-vediet}

- Respiratory diseases spread mainly through **droplets and aerosols** (coughing, sneezing, talking in enclosed spaces), less through touching contaminated surfaces. Intestinal infections spread through **dirty hands, water and food**.
- **Those most at risk** are older people, the chronically ill, people with weakened immunity, pregnant women and small children.
- **Reliable information** comes from your national public-health authority and health ministry, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) and the World Health Organization (WHO).
- Measures (masks, limits on gatherings, quarantine, isolation) change as the situation develops – check them regularly.

## During an epidemic {#pandemia-pocas}

### Protecting yourself and others {#pandemia-ochrana}

- **Wash your hands** with soap for at least 20 seconds – when you come home, before eating, after using the toilet, after coughing. If there is no water, use sanitiser with at least 60 % alcohol.
- **An FFP2 respirator** protects against inhaling aerosols – on public transport, in shops, in healthcare settings and when caring for a sick person. It must fit tightly.
- **Ventilate** – briefly and thoroughly several times a day, especially when there are several people in the room.
- **Keep your distance** from sick people and limit contact with people at risk.
- **Vaccination** as recommended – it protects against severe illness.

### If you or a household member falls ill {#pandemia-chory}

1. **Stay at home** and phone your doctor. Do not go to the surgery without calling first.
2. **Isolate the sick person** in one room, ideally with their own bathroom. One healthy household member looks after them.
3. **Ventilate** the sick person's room, wear a respirator when in contact, wash your hands.
4. **Keep separate** towels, dishes and bed linen. Clean frequently touched surfaces (door handles, switches, toilet).
5. **Monitor their condition**: temperature, breathing, fluid intake, urination. Write the values down.
6. **Drink enough** – fever, diarrhoea and vomiting can lead to dehydration, especially in children and older people. Use oral rehydration salts.

::: warning
**Call 112** (or your national ambulance number) if the sick person has **shortness of breath, chest pain or pressure, blue lips, confusion, cannot be woken, or is not passing urine**, or if a child has a fever with listlessness, a rash that does not fade under pressure, or seizures.
:::

### Mental strain and isolation {#pandemia-psychika}

Long-term restrictions and fear of illness are exhausting. Keep a **daily routine**, stay in touch with loved ones at least remotely, keep moving, and limit news to a few times a day. More in the chapter [Mental health](#hygiena-psychika).
